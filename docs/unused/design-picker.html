Three ways to read a case study

Each design renders the same content, parsed straight from the case-study markdown, as a visual, interactive, browsable page in the spirit of The Pudding's visual essays. Each is shown for Navier–Stokes and Long-Tail Research. They differ in how they keep readers interested without overwhelming them: the first paces the story, the second chunks it, the third layers it.

Design AScrollytelling

A · Scrollytelling essay

The classic Pudding format: a sticky graphic that changes as the story scrolls past.

  • Hero with a hook, verdict stamp and animated key numbers
  • Sticky timeline or pipeline that advances with each story step
  • Claims revealed one by one with a status bar; tap to see evidence
  • "Reading vs push back" side by side; debates with a "where do you stand?" slider that reveals the third framing
  • Full text always one "Read more" away

Best for: first-time readers and public audiences; cases with a strong timeline or process.

Design BStory cards

B · Story cards

One idea per card, like a guided quiz. Swipe, tap or use the arrow keys.

  • Chapter progress bar: Story · People · Claims · Risks · Deep dives · Debates · Decide · Takeaway
  • Claim check game: guess each claim's status, then see the evidence
  • Reveal-on-tap reading and push back; tabbed ethics issues
  • Vote on each debate; pick an option at the decision point
  • Final card sums up your score, votes and choice

Best for: classrooms and workshops, mobile, and readers who drift on long pages.

Design CLayered dossier

C · Layered dossier

A newspaper-style long read with a depth switch and a live appraisal scorecard.

  • Skim / Read / Deep switch: about 3 minutes, about 12 minutes, or everything with sources
  • "At a glance" dashboard: numbers, claim-status donut, timeline or pipeline
  • Filter claims by status; section tags show which checklist questions apply
  • Your appraisal sidebar: the 16 checklist questions highlight as you scroll; rate each and watch the sound, accountable and just meters fill

Best for: researchers and evaluators, repeat readers, and turning cases into tool evaluations.

How it works, and how to extend it to every case

Content comes from the markdown. docs/build.py parses every case file into docs/data/cases.js. After editing a case, run python3 docs/build.py and reload.

Visual extras are optional. docs/data/extras.js adds a hook, key numbers, short scroll steps, a timeline, pipeline or chart, and glossary terms per case. Cases without extras still render, using the background paragraphs and a claim-status chart.

Try other cases now. Any design accepts ?case= with alphafold, virtual-lab, undue-influence, morally-inadmissible or word-weaponization, for example a-scrolly.html?case=virtual-lab.

No build tools, no external requests. Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. It opens straight from disk and works on GitHub Pages. Reader answers, votes and scores are kept only in that reader's browser.
